# Milestone 6 — two purses, and draw

People, houses, and the two purses. Milestones 0–5 are unchanged on `monthSeed`. The fold and the staple market have not been started.

## What landed

`draw` is an effect. It moves a firm deposit to that firm's owner the way a person-to-person `pay` moves money: largest balance first, credit at the same bank, interbank untouched, the sum of deposit liabilities untouched. The flow row is `profitDraw`. The gate refuses a folded firm, a stranger owner, a non-positive amount, and a short purse. Applying a short `draw` anyway throws `LeakError`. That throw is a bug, not a refusal.

`openingPays` pays each worker's salary from the firm purse, in person-id order, and only when the purse covers the whole bill. It does not reach into the owner's deposits. `remainderDraws` runs after the loan step and sweeps a solvent firm's leftover cash to the owner. An insolvent firm is not paid a partial wage and is not drawn. That cash stays in the firm for the fold.

The household buys food with `pay` from its own purse. `household:cleo` on `purseSeed` asks for 20 of staple and is refused, `cleo has 0, short of 20`, while `shop` still holds 400. The firm is not a purse the leaf can name. Argument scope is `from === person`.

## Rulings

- A draw is not a pay to a bank. It cannot move interbank, and it cannot change the sum of deposit liabilities.
- Opening wages debit the firm. The owner's deposits are not a second pocket for that bill.
- Cash left in the firm is not food. The refusal is the household's own short purse.
- An insolvent firm is not drawn and does not pay a partial wage. The fold, not the owner, takes that estate.
